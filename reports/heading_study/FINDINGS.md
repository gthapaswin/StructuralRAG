# Heading study: findings so far

Question: when do section headings help retrieval inside a long document?
Full tables: [`results.md`](results.md) (generated by `scripts/analyze_heading_study.py`).

Setup: within-document section retrieval on four development question sets: GovReport ICT (1,179)
and LLM-written (398) questions, QASPER human questions (1,256), and Natural Questions real Google
queries over Wikipedia (1,744); plus a held-out fifth set, ConditionalQA (2,422, section 7). Structure-aware (SA) = RRF(BM25 over the structural view, cos(content) +
0.2·cos(heading path)); flat = RRF(BM25, dense) over section text only. Only headings change
between conditions. Settings come from GovReport dev; nothing is tuned on the test sets. Tests are
paired sign-flip tests with documents as the independent units (questions from the same document
are not independent), Holm-corrected within each question set.

## 1. Headings help, do nothing, or hurt, depending on the collection

| MRR@10 | flat | SA, real headings | SA, shuffled headings (placebo) | SA, generic "Section 3.2" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | 0.689 | 0.691 | 0.690 | 0.689 |
| GovReport LLM-written | 0.864 | 0.878 | 0.866 | 0.867 |
| QASPER | 0.509 | **0.542** | 0.507 | 0.507 |
| NQ | **0.642** | 0.616 | 0.624 | 0.631 |

- QASPER: real headings beat the placebo by +0.035 (p < 0.001). The gain comes from what headings
  say, not from extra text, and it shrinks steadily as headings are made generic or noisy (a
  dose-response curve: `figures/dose_response.png`).
- GovReport ICT: no effect (the question is a sentence copied from the section text).
- NQ: headings hurt (−0.026 vs flat, p < 0.001). (NQ headings were cleaned of Wikipedia "[edit]" link
  text and the page's contents box was removed; results barely changed.)

## 2. What explains it: how specific the target section's own heading is

Heading specificity w = log(N/df)/log N, where df is how many of the collection's N documents share
the heading ("Introduction" → about 0; a heading unique to its document → 1). No labels needed.

| SA gain over flat | generic target heading (w < 0.5) | unique target heading (w ≥ 0.9) | Spearman(w, gain) |
|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | −0.076 | +0.019 | 0.22 |
| GovReport LLM-written | −0.103 | +0.039 | 0.31 |
| QASPER | +0.003 | +0.057 | 0.18 |
| NQ | −0.056 | +0.034 | 0.15 |

All four correlations have p < 0.001. A section with a generic heading loses to neighbours whose
headings sound related to the question. NQ is negative overall because 63% of its targets have
generic headings (53% are the untitled lead section).

Robustness: in a regression of the per-question gain on the target's heading specificity plus its
section length, depth, relative position and the document's size (standardized; 95% CIs from a
bootstrap over documents), specificity stays positive with a CI above zero in every question set
(pooled +0.031 per standard deviation, CI 0.023–0.038; `results.md` section J). Real examples:
[`examples.md`](examples.md).

Replication with a 3× larger encoder (bge-base-en-v1.5, same settings): the same pattern in all four
sets. Gain for generic vs unique target headings: GovReport ICT −0.072 / +0.013, LLM-written
−0.096 / +0.036, QASPER −0.004 / +0.053, NQ −0.058 / +0.041 (Spearman 0.18–0.28, all p < 0.001).
Real headings beat the placebo only on QASPER (p < 0.001), and still hurt on NQ (−0.025 vs flat).

Replication with a 10× larger encoder (bge-large-en-v1.5, all five question sets including the
held-out ConditionalQA): the same pattern again. Generic vs unique target headings: GovReport ICT
−0.076 / +0.011, LLM-written −0.119 / +0.045, QASPER −0.012 / +0.049, NQ −0.067 / +0.023,
ConditionalQA −0.063 / +0.015 (Spearman 0.14–0.31, all p < 0.001). SA vs flat: QASPER +0.022
(p < 0.001), NQ −0.034 (p < 0.001), the rest not significant. The role-neutral method again beats
SA on ConditionalQA (+0.008, p < 0.001) and NQ (+0.007, p < 0.001), and is level elsewhere. So the
effects are not an artefact of a small encoder (section K of `results.md`).
Paper figures: `figures/paper/`.

## 3. Negative results (reported, not hidden)

- A document-level heading-informativeness score (does each heading point to its own section?) does
  not predict which documents gain (all |Spearman| < 0.13), and setting the path weight per document
  from it, or per query with a learned gate, does not beat a fixed weight. This matches Wu & Lin
  (2026) on query-adaptive fusion weights. Per-document and per-question oracles show large headroom
  (+0.07 to +0.10 MRR), so the right weight varies, but these signals do not find it.
- A label-free fix that makes generic headings neutral (path score replaced by the document mean,
  weighted by 1 − w) helps where common headings carry little meaning (GovReport LLM-written +0.009,
  NQ +0.008, both significant before Holm correction; NQ also after) but costs −0.012 on QASPER, where common headings
  ("Results", "Datasets") are exactly what questions target. Heading frequency predicts where SA
  loses, but frequency alone cannot tell a boilerplate heading from a functional one.
- The GovReport-fine-tuned encoder keeps most of the QASPER heading gain (+0.027 vs +0.033).
- Human-reviewed labels do not fix it either. 210 frequent headings were pre-labelled by the local
  LLM and reviewed by the author (7 of 210 changed, all among rows flagged as likely errors; a
  second, independent labeller would strengthen this). Making only *boilerplate* headings neutral
  keeps the QASPER gain intact (0.542, unchanged) but no longer helps GovReport (+0.001) and
  slightly hurts NQ (−0.006). The losses do not come from boilerplate headings (GAO Contact,
  References), which are rarely targets.

## 4. Where the losses come from: summary sections

Among questions whose target heading is generic, the losses concentrate in sections that summarise
the document: in NQ the untitled lead ("Introduction") accounts for −59 of the −61.5 total MRR lost;
in GovReport ICT, "Conclusions" (−5.2), "Background" (−3.5) and "Introduction" (−1.3) lead; in QASPER
"Introduction" (−6.2) and "Conclusion" (−1.0). These sections restate content that body sections
cover under specific headings, so a question is pulled to the body section whose heading matches it
("when did the first Harry Potter movie come out" → "Release" instead of the lead).

Is that only an evaluation artifact (the body section also answers the question)? NQ's short answers
allow a check (1,467 questions with a short answer): the top-1 section contains the answer in 69.4%
of questions for flat retrieval vs 67.6% for structure-aware (−0.018, p = 0.03), close to the gold-
section gap (−0.020). Single-gold labels are strict for both methods, but the loss is real.
(`nq_answer_check.json`)

## 5. Position against the closest work (both read in full)

- Kuehlkamp et al. (arXiv 2610.10170, v1 Oct 2026): placebo-controlled heading-path prefixes for
  chunk retrieval over whole corpora (QASPER, Wikipedia; bge-small/large). Real paths beat shuffled
  ones by +0.010 to +0.016 cov-nDCG@10. They analyse depth and query type only: not which headings
  help, not heading frequency, and they report no case of real headings hurting.
- STAIR (arXiv 2609.03874, v2 Oct 2026, IBM): fine-tuned LLM that generates the ToC leaf for a query
  within one book (18 textbooks, LLM-written questions); no public datasets, no study of heading
  quality.
- This study adds: within-document section routing on public data with human questions, a case where
  real headings hurt (NQ), and the target-heading specificity account of when they help.

## 6. LLM-written headings: a test of the summary-section account

A local LLM (qwen3:8b) wrote a short, specific heading for every section of a fixed random sample of
documents (197 GovReport, 199 QASPER, 245 NQ), seeing only each section's first 60 words, never the
questions (`results.md` section G; rewritten documents only):

| SA gain over flat | summary-section targets: authors' headings | LLM headings everywhere | LLM headings on summary sections only | other targets: authors' | LLM everywhere | LLM on summary only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | −0.118 | −0.030 | −0.042 | +0.011 | +0.003 | +0.010 |
| GovReport LLM-written | −0.113 | +0.015 | −0.013 | +0.025 | +0.016 | +0.022 |
| QASPER | −0.044 | −0.023 | −0.010 | +0.052 | +0.019 | +0.017 |
| NQ | −0.060 | −0.029 | +0.013 | −0.009 | −0.026 | −0.023 |

- Giving summary sections (Introduction / lead / Abstract / Conclusions / Background) a heading that
  names their content shrinks or removes their losses in every set, as the account predicts.
- The LLM's headings are worse than the authors' specific headings for other sections (QASPER
  +0.052 → +0.019 with LLM headings everywhere).
- Rewriting only the summary headings (exploratory: chosen after seeing the results above) removes
  NQ's loss (+0.032 over the authors' headings, p = 0.001; now level with flat, −0.004, n.s.) and
  helps GovReport slightly (+0.004 to +0.006, n.s.), but costs QASPER −0.021 (p < 0.001): once the
  Abstract and Introduction carry specific headings, they pull questions away from body sections,
  even though the body sections' headings did not change.
- Sections compete for a question. A heading change helps its own section and hurts its neighbours,
  so whether a repair pays off depends on where a collection's questions point: at summaries (NQ's
  lead) or at body sections (QASPER).

## 7. Held-out test on a fourth domain (pre-registered)

Predictions for ConditionalQA (495 gov.uk guidance pages, 2,422 crowd-written questions) were
written, approved and pushed before any retrieval was run on it
([`heldout_predictions.md`](heldout_predictions.md), commit 6f8637c). Results
([`heldout_results.md`](heldout_results.md)), document-clustered tests:

| | Prediction | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | SA beats flat (+0.02 to +0.03) | −0.005 [−0.015, +0.005] | fails |
| P2 | Real beats shuffled headings | +0.003 [−0.005, +0.011] | fails |
| P3 | Gain rises with target specificity | Spearman 0.03, p = 0.12 | fails |
| P4 | Summary-only targets lose | −0.207 (n = 74) | holds |
| P5 | Unique targets gain more than generic ones | −0.004 vs −0.082 | holds |
| P6 | Role-neutral method not worse than SA | +0.011 [+0.007, +0.014], p < 0.001 | holds |
| P7 | Role-neutral beats SA on summary-only targets | +0.125, p < 0.001 | holds |

- **The harm side generalizes; the benefit side does not.** Summary sections lose heavily on the
  new domain, as predicted, and the gap between generic and unique targets reappears. But specific
  headings gave no gain here (unique targets −0.004), so the predicted overall benefit failed. The
  explanation first offered (questions describe personal situations and do not use heading words)
  was tested afterwards and is **not supported** (section 10: ConditionalQA questions use their
  target heading's words more often than QASPER's). The specificity account explains where
  structure-aware retrieval loses, not on its own whether it wins.
- **A definitional note on P3 (reported, not used to rescue it).** The pre-registration scored each
  question by its *most* specific gold section, because half of ConditionalQA's questions have
  several. The analyses of the other four sets use the first gold section. Under that definition
  ConditionalQA does show the predicted correlation (Spearman 0.146, p < 0.001; generic targets
  −0.080, unique +0.017). This was computed after the test and is post hoc; P3 as registered fails.
- **The role-neutral method works out of sample.** Frozen before the test, it is the best system on
  ConditionalQA (MRR@10 0.706 vs SA 0.695 and flat 0.700); +0.011 over SA (p < 0.001), level with
  flat (+0.005, n.s.). Against flat across all five question sets: QASPER +0.028 and GovReport
  LLM-written +0.022 (both p < 0.05), ConditionalQA and GovReport ICT level, NQ −0.015 (the keyword
  side still uses headings there).

## 8. Does the retrieval difference change the final answer?

400 random questions each from NQ (short answers) and ConditionalQA (span answers; yes/no
excluded). Each system's top-3 sections go to the same LLM (qwen3:8b), which writes a cited answer;
correct = the answer contains a reference string (`answers.md`).

| | flat | SA | role-neutral |
|---|---|---|---|
| NQ: gold section in top-3 | 0.750 | 0.738 | 0.748 |
| NQ: answer correct | 0.613 | 0.615 | 0.618 |
| ConditionalQA: gold section in top-3 | 0.777 | 0.795 | 0.800 |
| ConditionalQA: answer correct | 0.487 | 0.500 | 0.512 |

The order of the systems is the same as in retrieval (role-neutral ≥ SA, flat; on ConditionalQA
role-neutral − flat = +0.025), but no difference in answer correctness is significant (all
document-clustered p ≥ 0.12; role-neutral vs SA on ConditionalQA +0.013, 95% CI [0.000, +0.028]).
Reading the top 3 sections absorbs most of the top-1 differences, and 400 questions can only detect
effects of about ±0.03. Honest reading: the method's retrieval gains are real but small, and at
this sample size they do not show up as a measurable change in final answers. Also, on NQ the
retrieval loss of SA (MRR −0.026) does not turn into worse answers when 3 sections are read.

## 9. Finding summary sections without reading their headings

The role-neutral method finds summary sections with a fixed English list of heading words. Two
label-free rules that never read heading text were compared, the rule chosen on GovReport dev only
(`label_free_roles.md`): **position** (the document's first and last top-level sections) and
**centrality** (the section most similar to the rest of its document).

| MRR@10 | flat | SA | heading list | position (first + last) | centrality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | 0.689 | 0.691 | 0.692 | 0.693 | 0.692 |
| GovReport LLM-written | 0.864 | 0.878 | 0.886 | 0.881 | 0.872 |
| QASPER | 0.509 | 0.542 | 0.537 | 0.534 | 0.539 |
| NQ | 0.642 | 0.616 | 0.627 | 0.627 | 0.619 |
| ConditionalQA | 0.700 | 0.695 | 0.706 | 0.705 | 0.700 |

- The position rule does as well as the heading list in every set (differences −0.005 to +0.001,
  all p ≥ 0.1) and, like it, beats SA on NQ (+0.011) and ConditionalQA (+0.009, both p < 0.001),
  with a cost on QASPER (−0.008 vs SA, p < 0.001; the list: −0.005, p = 0.08). So the method does
  not depend on English heading words: summary roles can be read from document position.
- Centrality is a poor detector (the section it picks is on the list only 7–52% of the time; 7% on
  NQ, where every document's first section is the lead) and does not help.
- What the position rule relies on: in QASPER, NQ and most ConditionalQA pages the first section is
  the summary by format (Abstract 402/402, lead 1,744/1,744, Overview 312/495), so there it agrees
  with the list largely by construction. On GovReport it finds only 39% of list sections and is
  still level with the list (where the method changes little anyway). It would not transfer to
  documents that do not open with a summary.
- Caveats: GovReport dev barely separates the rules (position+last 0.704, position-first and the
  list 0.703), and ConditionalQA had been used before, so this is a development result, not a
  pre-registered test.

## 10. Do headings help only when the question uses their words?

For each question, does at least one content word of the target section's heading occur in the
question, and is the target's heading path the closest one in the document to the question
(`question_heading_overlap.md`)?

| | heading word in question | heading points to target | SA gain: with / without a heading word | Spearman(overlap, gain) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | 58% | 28% | +0.027 / −0.034 | 0.18 |
| GovReport LLM-written | 75% | 48% | +0.044 / −0.076 | 0.22 |
| QASPER | 19% | 29% | +0.112 / +0.014 | 0.19 |
| NQ | 10% | 19% | +0.068 / −0.036 | 0.15 |
| ConditionalQA | 38% | 24% | +0.057 / −0.043 | 0.19 |

- In every set, structure-aware retrieval gains when the question shares a word with the target's
  heading and mostly loses when it does not (all correlations p < 0.001). Among unique target
  headings only, the correlation stays in four of five sets (0.11–0.19; not on GovReport
  LLM-written), so word match adds to specificity rather than repeating it.
- The ConditionalQA explanation in section 7 is refuted: its questions use heading words more
  often than QASPER's (38% vs 19%). What differs is the cost when they do not match (−0.043 vs
  +0.014 on QASPER). A likely reason (not tested separately): a non-matching question is pulled to a
  neighbour whose heading sounds related.

## 11. Does a cross-encoder reranker show the same summary-section loss?

bge-reranker-base (zero-shot) rescores the top 20 sections of flat retrieval, reading each section
in 200-word windows, once as text only and once with its heading path in front
(`reranker_headings.md`). Role-neutral here means summary-role sections are read without their
heading path.

| | flat | rerank: text only | rerank: path + text | role-neutral (list) | role-neutral (position) | heading gain: summary targets / others |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GovReport ICT | 0.689 | 0.638 | 0.683 | 0.678 | 0.677 | +0.032 / +0.046 |
| GovReport LLM-written | 0.864 | 0.848 | 0.916 | 0.912 | 0.914 | +0.010 / +0.074 |
| QASPER | 0.509 | 0.460 | 0.522 | 0.570 | 0.575 | −0.045 / +0.082 |
| NQ | 0.642 | 0.680 | 0.704 | 0.704 | 0.705 | +0.019 / +0.029 |
| ConditionalQA | 0.700 | 0.732 | 0.783 | 0.776 | 0.766 | +0.059 / +0.051 |

- In the cross-encoder, headings help on every set, including NQ and ConditionalQA (+0.024 to
  +0.068, all p < 0.001), where they did not help first-stage retrieval (NQ −0.026, ConditionalQA
  −0.005 n.s.). Reading the question and the
  heading together seems to let the model judge whether the heading is relevant instead of being
  pulled by any similar-sounding heading.
- The zero-shot reranker itself is not always better than flat first-stage retrieval: on GovReport
  ICT it is slightly below (0.683 with paths vs 0.689; not tested for significance), where the
  questions are sentences copied from the text.
- The summary-section loss largely disappears: headings help summary-section targets too on NQ
  (+0.019, p = 0.004) and ConditionalQA (+0.059, p = 0.07). The exception is QASPER (−0.045,
  p = 0.08), where summary targets are the abstract and introduction.
- Role-neutral reading therefore helps only on QASPER (+0.048 list, +0.053 position, p < 0.001); it
  makes no difference on NQ and GovReport LLM-written and costs a little on GovReport ICT (−0.005)
  and ConditionalQA (−0.007 list, −0.017 position, p < 0.001).
- The QASPER gain (+0.05) is larger than what the 198 summary-section targets alone can plausibly
  account for (their heading loss is −0.045, i.e. about −0.007 over all 1,256 questions), so it
  probably comes mostly from other targets (per-question scores were not saved to confirm this): reading abstracts and introductions without their heading
  makes them weaker competitors. Caveat: the role-neutral variant mixes scores from two inputs
  (with and without path), which the model may not score on the same scale.
- Conclusion: the summary-section problem belongs to similarity-based (bi-encoder and BM25)
  retrieval, not to headings as such. A cross-encoder mostly handles heading roles itself, and the
  role-neutral rule should be applied in the first stage, not in the reranker. Zero-shot only; a
  reranker fine-tuned with heading paths was not tested here.
